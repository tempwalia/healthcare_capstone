TITLE: How a Referral Works, Start to Finish
CATEGORY: Referral Process
AUDIENCE: Patients, primary care providers, specialists, care coordinators

This guide explains what happens after a referral is submitted on the Intelligent Care
Coordination platform, in plain language, stage by stage. Every stage below matches a real
status you will see on the referral's detail page.

1. SUBMITTED
   The referral has been created — a primary care provider (or the patient themselves, or a care
   coordinator on their behalf) has recorded the reason for the referral, the requesting doctor,
   and optionally a preferred specialist or location. The platform takes over automatically from
   here; nobody needs to "process" it by hand.

2. REVIEWING DOCUMENTS (intake_processing / awaiting_documents)
   The platform checks for a referral letter and a recent imaging or lab report. If both a reason
   and at least one document are present, this step also extracts diagnosis and procedure codes
   automatically. If neither a reason nor any document has been provided yet, the referral pauses
   here — waiting on the patient or referring doctor to add a reason or upload a document. Nothing
   else can happen until this step has something to work with.

3. VERIFYING INSURANCE COVERAGE (eligibility_checking)
   The patient's insurance policy number is checked against the payer's network and prior
   authorization rules. This happens automatically and normally takes moments, not days. See
   `prior_authorization_policy.txt` for what "prior authorization required" means, and the
   `insurance/` documents in this knowledge base for what each specific plan covers.

   If verification does not pass (INSURANCE VERIFICATION DID NOT PASS / eligibility_denied), the
   referral pauses and a care coordinator is notified to review it — a denial is not automatically
   the end of the referral. A coordinator can override the result after review (for example, if
   the policy number was mistyped, or a manual authorization was obtained outside the system) and
   the referral continues from exactly where it left off.

4. SELECTING A SPECIALIST (awaiting_specialist_approval)
   Once eligibility is confirmed, the platform recommends one or more specialists based on
   diagnosis, location, and insurance network — each with the reasoning behind the recommendation
   (network status, distance, rating, next available slot). This is a human-in-the-loop step: a
   care coordinator or specialist reviews the candidates and confirms one before anything is
   booked. The platform never books an appointment with a specialist nobody has approved.

5. BOOKING THE APPOINTMENT (scheduling)
   Once a specialist is confirmed, the platform automatically finds the next available slot with
   that specialist and books it. If no slot is available within the target wait time, this step
   flags the referral for a coordinator to consider an alternative.

6. APPOINTMENT SCHEDULED (scheduled)
   The patient has a confirmed appointment. The referring doctor and the specialist can both see
   the appointment and every document/history attached to the referral.

7. REFERRAL COMPLETED (completed)
   After the appointment happens, the treating doctor (or a care coordinator relaying the visit)
   records the consult outcome — symptoms, diagnosis, and any prescription. The platform then
   automatically generates a whole-care-journey summary covering the referring doctor's original
   reason, the specialist's findings, and prior history, visible to everyone who was part of the
   referral. The referring doctor should be followed up with as advised in that summary.

WHO IS "WAITING ON WHOM"
Most stages (document review, eligibility checking, appointment booking) run automatically and
need nobody to act. The two stages that need a human are: a denied eligibility check (waiting on
care coordination staff) and specialist selection (waiting on a coordinator or specialist to
confirm a candidate). If a referral seems stuck, check whether it's sitting at one of those two
stages — that's almost always why.
