TITLE: Prior Authorization — When It's Required and What It Means
CATEGORY: Eligibility & Insurance
AUDIENCE: Patients, care coordinators, payer staff

WHAT PRIOR AUTHORIZATION MEANS HERE
Prior authorization means the payer requires sign-off before a specific procedure is covered,
separate from basic eligibility (whether the plan is active and in-network). A referral can be
fully "verified" and in-network and still be marked as requiring prior authorization — those are
two different checks.

WHICH REFERRALS TRIGGER IT
On this platform, prior authorization is required whenever the referral's procedure code falls
under a spine or musculoskeletal category — specifically ICD-10 codes beginning with M51 (spine
disc disorders, e.g. a herniated disc) or M54 (back pain / dorsalgia). This is a deliberately
narrow, illustrative rule matching the mock payer system's own logic, not a claim that only these
two categories ever require authorization with a real insurer.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT'S REQUIRED
The eligibility check still returns "verified" if the plan and network status are otherwise fine —
prior_auth_required is a flag alongside that result, not a denial. It's informational: the care
team should be aware that formal authorization paperwork may be needed with the real payer before
or alongside the visit, in addition to the platform's own referral approval steps.

HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM A DENIED ELIGIBILITY CHECK
A denied eligibility check (unrecognized policy number, out-of-network plan) stops the referral
and routes it to a care coordinator for review before anything else can happen. A prior-auth flag
does not stop the referral — it proceeds through specialist selection and scheduling normally,
with the flag visible on the referral record as a reminder.
